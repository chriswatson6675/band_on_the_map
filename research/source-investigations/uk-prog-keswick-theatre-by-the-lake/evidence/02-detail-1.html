The Farmer’s Wife

A new play by Hannah Khalil | Inspired by the memoir by Helen Rebanks Friday 18 September - Saturday 10 October 2026 | 7.30pm; with 2pm matinees on selected dates

Welcome to the farm.

In The Farmer’s Wife, one day holds a lifetime. Three generations of women from the same family – a daughter, a mother and a grandmother – share a kitchen, their stories overlapping as the past and present sit side by side.

The animals need feeding. The bills need paying. The forms need filling in. The meals need cooking. The vet needs calling. The list never ends.

At the centre of it all is one woman, holding everything together and beginning to wonder how much longer she can.

Inspired by Helen Rebanks’ Sunday Times best-selling memoir and shaped by the voices of women in farming across Cumbria, this new play captures both the beauty and the weight of a life rooted in the land.

By turns honest, moving and surprising, The Farmer’s Wife is a story of resilience and community in a changing world.

We do post-show discussions for every Theatre by the Lake production. Stay a little longer: films & post-show conversations

The story doesn’t end when the curtain comes down.On selected dates throughout the run of The Farmer’s Wife, join us after the performance for a special programme of post-show discussions and screenings of ROOTED HERE, our new short film celebrating some of the real women and farming stories that helped inform the production.

Hear from Helen Rebanks, Hannah Khalil, Liz Stevenson, Anna Jones, Amy Bateman, James Rebanks, Amy Hughes, Maria Benjamin, Ruby Nicholson and members of the company and women from Cumbria’s farming community as we explore farming, family, creativity and the journey from real lives to the stage.

CREATIVES

Helen Rebanks - Author of The Farmer's Wife

Helen Rebanks is a farmer, author, and advocate for sustainable agriculture, rooted in the landscape and traditions of the English Lake District. Shaped by a childhood on a Cumbrian farm, where her family has worked the land for generations — her family farmed dairy cows, beef cattle, and sheep, and her grandfather bred pedigree Clydesdale horses — she brings both heritage and lived experience to her writing.

A graduate of Fine Art with first-class honours, Helen worked in Oxford before returning to Cumbria, where she farms alongside her husband and raises four children. Her Sunday Times instant bestselling memoir, The Farmer's Wife, is a compelling account of modern rural life — honest, grounded, and deeply human.

Hannah Khalil - Writer

Hannah Khalil is Writer in Residence at Bristol Old Vic (2025-2030). Recent work includes critically acclaimed My English Persian Kitchen which had a sell out run at the Traverse as part of the Edinburgh Festival in August 2024, then transferred to Soho Theatre, London followed by a UK and Irish tour. It opens at Atlas in Washington DC this autumn in a Culture Ireland funded production. 

Hannah was the 2022 Resident Writer at Shakespeare’s Globe, while there the Globe produced three of her plays: Hakawatis: Women of the Arabian Nights, Henry VIII and The Fir Tree (2021 and 2022). Hannah’s other stage plays include A Museum in Baghdad (Royal Shakespeare Company) which marked the first play by a woman of Arab heritage on a main stage at the RSC,  Interference (National Theatre of Scotland) and the critically acclaimed Scenes from 68* Years - shortlisted for the James Tait Black Award (Arcola Theatre, London, 2016). Her first libretto for a new Key Stage 2 Opera called The Great Stink toured the UK in 2024 with English Touring Opera. 

Hannah held the Heimbold Chair of Irish Studies at Villanova University in 2021 and was a Creative Fellow of the Samuel Beckett Archive for 2021/2022. She co-conceived and acted as co-artistic director for the Not Beckett international festival of new plays. She is currently an Honorary Fellow of Birkbeck University. In 2022 she was made a Fellow of the Royal Society of Literature. She is an Associate Artist of Shakespeare’s Globe.

Liz is a recipient of the JMK Young Director Award. She was formerly the co-founder and Artistic Director of Junction 8 Theatre, making new plays with, for and about the community in her hometown of Chorley, Lancashire (Under the Market Roof and Lancastrians).

The Bradford Improv Theatre

BigImprov!

Bradford's biggest impro theatre. Open to everyone. Bringing people of Bradford and beyond together, unlocking their creativity, confidence and communication. Bringing people together, enriching their lives with classes, workshops, and performances. Since 2017.

Why Improv?

Improv is theatre without scripts.

You'll play games, invent stories, laugh a lot and surprise yourself.

Along the way you'll become a better listener, a more confident communicator and much more comfortable thinking on your feet.

The open session.

We have an open public improv drop in.
Friendly, accessible, no experience necessary, the open session is exactly what it says it is. You can drop in or out whenever you like with no obligation whatsoever. You can opt in or out of any activity, although we do encourage you to challenge yourself. Visit us at the Carr Lane Club (39 Carr Lane, Shipley, BD18 2NQ). Every Tuesday, 7.20pm. Pay as you feel, £5 if you've got it, don't worry if you haven't.

Whatever your interest or level of experience, you are welcome. If you wish to perform you'll need to join the performance group. Membership of the performance group is by audition. If you wish to audition for the performance group then we'll support you, if you don't then that's perfectly fine. Most members of Bigimprov are not members of the performance group.

The Performance Group

Relax, you won't be going on stage unless you want to. Auditions for the performance group are held a couple of times a year and priority is given to people who've attended drop ins and other sessions. If you wish to go on to audition for the performance group you're welcome to. If you don't then that's perfectly fine.

Shows

The Performance Group have performed in a range of projects. They've done silly comedy, they've run scratch nights sharing the stage with new and experimental performance work, and they've done turns at comedy nights.

They've done comedy short form improv (the sort of thing you'll see on tv's 'Whose Line is it Anyway'), and they've done spellbinding longform improv, spontaneously creating entire plays in front of an audience. They're a versatile bunch.

If you'd like to join them then you can start the process by coming along to our open nights. If you'd like to book them (or you have any questions) then get in touch

About Impro(v)

You stand in a room with a bunch of people who, just a few short weeks ago were complete strangers.

Now you know each other. You've created together, played together. Maybe you're becoming friends, maybe not, but these are people you've created with, these are people you can trust.

You make a story offer to your scene partner. They accept your offer and move the story forward, just a little. The two of you repeat this process and an entire story emerges, with plot and character, one gentle step at a time. It's like magic.

You have gone from walking into the room wondering what on Earth you're doing there, to being an actor in a play that you are co-writing with trusted collaborators. In a support of a positive group you are being creative. You are writing, you are acting. It's easy.

You play silly games. Except they're not silly games - mammals play for serious reasons. They are exercises honed over generations to develop essential skills. Seriously, ask your improv teacher. Any teacher worthy of the name can tell you exactly which creative skills an exercise is developing.

You're laughing, and in laughter you're pushing back the boundaries of yourself, exercising those parts of the brain that deal with change and flexibility. You're learning, too, that failure can be fun, a vital part of learning. As you play you grow. At any age.

You walk out of every session with the buzz of having worked your imagination, of having flexed your creative muscles. Over time you will find yourself more confident, more creative, better able to deal with change. If you create in other fields then you'll find the workout your imagination gets in impro spills over into them. If you don't then impro will give you the confidence to try.

Find Us.

The Carr Lane Club (39 Carr Lane, Shipley, BD18 2NQ) is on the edge of Shipley, just north of Bradford, a short walk from Wrose, Idle, and Thackley, maybe half a mile from Saltaire. It has generous parking, there's a bustop right outside the venue and one just down the hill, and it's an eight minute walk from Shipley train station, which has direct trains to Bradford and Leeds every fifteen minutes, and direct services to Skipton, Bingley, Keighley, and Ilkley. It's pretty well connected.

The Carr Lane is a private member's club, so if you want to keep coming (and we hope you will) then you will need to join as a member. Membership of the centre is currently £12 a year for under 60s, £6 a year over. Your membership gets you your own door card and a discount at the bar. It also thanks what has so far been a truly superb host.

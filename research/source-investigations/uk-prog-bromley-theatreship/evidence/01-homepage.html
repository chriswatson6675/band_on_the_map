WELCOME ABOARD!


Our Mission


To create a world-class arts centre on a historic cargo ship in the old docks of Canary Wharf

Our Vision


Built in 1913 when London’s docks were filled with similar ships. It’s now one of the last ones left here. Restored and lovingly converted into a public arts centre the ship has a new lease of life. The cargo hold now holds a 100-seater theatre, a characterful bar, and an exhibition space.

It’s a unique space, in a really special part of London, and we really hope our programming reflects some of that. The docks have always introduced new things to London, and we’re keen to continue that history with events that highlight new and interesting voices, and bring new audiences to more established artists.

There's a special alchemy in ports - where people and ideas come from all over the world and mix - it's part of what makes East London so special and our project hopes to mirror that. We aim for an events programme where people and ideas can continue to mix. We want to provide a space for the interdisciplinary, the underepresented, and the experimental, and a shared social space for people to meet and connect.

In celebrating the rich history of this area we try to look forwards: whether that's through new recompositions of ancient folk tunes, contemporary performances paired with classic films, or through loving repairs to keep the ship alive for another hundred years. Most of all we hope to create a space that feels welcoming - and we can't wait to welcome you onboard!

Our Values


Heritage: We want to celebrate the rich history of the area by opening a public arts centre onboard a living piece of its history.

Diversity: We are committed to developing an arts programme whose range and quality reflect the brilliant diversity of this location and its inhabitants.

Environment: We aim to enhance Canary Wharf’s environment, ecologically and socially, and to serve as a beacon of sustainability.

Community: We hope to enhance the social, cultural and artistic life of the area, by creating a public arts venue which benefits local workers and residents and attracts visitors from across London. We want to remain welcoming and friendly, with a focus on providing shared social space.

Accessibility: This project is committed to accessibility: broadening access to the arts through programming choices and the warmth of our team, removing financial barriers with our no-questions asked discounted tickets policy, and aiming to make our space as physically accessible as we can.

Curators

Inigo Lapwood

Founding Director

It's no secret that Inigo loves boats. He also loves making and fixing things - ranging from old ship engines, to immersive events, to new language models.

For over ten years Inigo’s work has focussed on the restoration and repair of historic steel ships. Having overseen the full restoration of seven ships, including two on the National Historic Ships register, Inigo is passionately committed to restoring the heritage of our waterways. Inigo is a member of the Council of Experts for National Historic Ships UK, and sits on the London Advisory Board for the Canal and River Trust.

Inigo also works as a creative producer in immersive event production - for the past three years he's been Theatrical Ground Crew Director for Boomtown Festival - the world's largest immersive theatre event.

Additionally, Inigo works as an AI Developer and data-scientist - specialising in natural language analysis. Most recently as lead-developer and a founding director at strategy consultancy R-ai. R-ai takes seriously the idea of listening - talking to every employee in large organisations to help democratise strategic decision making. R-ai has developed an impressive reputation for transforming the effectiveness of large organisations across sectors and across the world. You can read more about Inigo's work at R-ai here.

At Theatreship, Inigo is excited to be bringing these strands of his professional interests together - physically restoring a historic cargo ship, leading on the programming to open the ship up as a public space for innovative events, and grounding the project in active listening to build a project that truly responds to the needs of the people around it. If you see him onboard come say hi!

If you'd like to read more about his various projects outside of Theatreship you can find some if it here.

Natalie Hill

Director & Head of Film Programming

When Nat was writing her diary at 5 years old, every page started with 'Today I watched Television'. 40 years later she's a multi-BAFTA winning TV director & producer. Over her 25 year career she's produced some of the most significant shows in Britain, including the Apprentice, 24 hours in A&E, Great British Menu, Stacey Dooley investigates, & Fantastical Food with Heston Blumenthal.

Nat was Creative Director at Rogo films/AJH Media - producers of 'Unprecedented' a ground-breaking documentary with exclusive access to the Trump family, and Grierson shortlisted Bad Boy Chiller Crew.

Most recently she developed and executive produced Netflix documentary series 'The Man with 1000 Kids' exploring the unregulated world of rogue sperm donation. Released last year to critical acclaim the series became Netflix's most viewed show in 95 countries.

Nat brings years of experience in film and TV to her role as head of film programming at Theatreship.

At Theatreship Nat's been critical in developing partnerships with BFI - delivering four BFI funded film festivals to date. She leads the film curation onboard, while managing to be an indispensable part of everything else that happens here too! Expect to see her at our weekly Free Mystery Cinema events where she'll be introducing cinematic gems and sharing what she loves about these films.

Want to read more about Nat and what she's worked on? You can find out more here.

Abi Asisa

Sapphic Cinema

Abi is a London-based producer and electroacoustic cellist whose work blends classical, improvisational, and experimental influences. Through layering and live manipulation, she transforms the cello into a source of textured, immersive soundscapes that move fluidly between composition and improvisation.

She has performed at venues including Café OTO, the Southbank Centre, the Tate Modern, and is an alumni of the Montreux Jazz artists residency.

Onboard Theatreship, alongside her performances, she curates and hosts our monthly 'Sapphic Cinema' night: London's only regular FLINTA film club. Each month pairing iconic films with new live performances. You can read more about Abi's work onboard here.

Ignacio Salvadores

contemporary and experimental music

Hailing from Argentina, Ignacio is a multi-instrumentalist, vocalist, and composer, now based in London. Perhaps best known as a core collaborator and live band member for Archy Marshall (King Krule), Ignacio's saxophone playing was instrumental in shaping the sonic direction of the Mercury Prize-nominated album The Ooz, as well as Man Alive! and Space Heavy.

Alongside his work with Archy, Ignacio releases and performs experimental solo music under 'Gal Go', blending jazz, post-punk, and electronic influences with vocals sung in his native Spanish.

Onboard Theatreship, Ignacio and Archy curate the incredible, intimate 'Something Atleast' nights. It's one of our favourite nights onboard - it's where you can find some of the most creative voices in London if you just know where to look. They have a real knack for bringing incredible creative talent together. Expect anything from Argentine trap pioneer Lara91k, experimental performance art meets hip hop in Slauson Malone, to YAK/Okay Kaya's new project: Beguiling Junior.

Ignacio also recently led a breathtaking, eery, live score performance for the 1911 Italian silent film L'Inferno, as part of our BFI-supported programme of silent films with experimental new live scores.

When not on the ship, he can be found here.

Vincent

experimental and acoustic

Vincent is a London-based trumpeter, composer, and audio engineer. Deeply rooted in the London alternative scene, Vincent's worked as both live performer and audio producer for bands including Fat White Family, Muckspreader, and Pet Deaths.

Drawing inspiration from the ambient, Fourth World music of Jon Hassell, Vincent is the creative force behind the musical project Body Orchestra. He crafts intricate, atmospheric compositions that blend live instrumentation with video art and sculptural set design.

Vincent's work with Body Orchestra has brought entirely new dimensions to Theatreship's programming. His original live score for soviet classic Battleship Potemkin remains one of our favourite nights onboard to date. His score blended samples from Shostakovich's 11th, itself memorialising the events depicted in the theme - then reconstituted through tape loop, distortion, and the live performance of the 5 piece Body Orchestra.

Vincent's multidisciplinary approach perfectly embodies the experimental spirit of our events.

Vincent curates the regular 'Acoustic' Music nights onboard. A night that started as low-key unplugged sets in the bar, the inverted commas have come to denote a far greater breadth of programming than the word 'acoustic' can contain. It's a home for the new, the bold, and the experimental.

A brilliant performer in his own right, you'll often also find him behind the sound-desk onboard, making everyone else sound great too.

Naomi Banks

Jazz and Soul

Naomi Banks is a London-based vocalist and composer. Naomi earned a scholarship to the Purcell School of Music—studying voice, piano, and saxophone—before completing a jazz voice scholarship at the Trinity Laban Conservatoire.

Deeply rooted in the contemporary alternative soul scene, she has provided live support for critically acclaimed artists including Celeste and Rachel Chinouriri, and performed alongside Laura Mvula and the ELO.

Naomi is the pioneer of her self-coined genre, "neon soul"—a fusion and deconstruction of jazz, pop, and electronica. Following the breakout success of her debut EP Deja Vu and playlisting and support from BBC Radio 1, and fresh off the back of the role of Orpheus in Punch Drunk's Burnt City she has cemented herself as one of the UK's most exciting boundary-pushing vocalists.

Onboard Theatreship, Naomi is a vital part of our live music curation. If you were lucky enough to catch our BFI Melodrama screening of The Umbrellas of Cherbourg, you will have heard her stunning voice first-hand in her live multi-lingual performance responding to the classic film.

Naomi is responsible for much of the Jazz and Soul programme onboard.

Owen Spafford

folk music

A virtuoso on the fiddle and a deeply thoughtful composer, Owen has spent his life immersed in the traditional music of England and Ireland. He first gained national recognition as an original member of the National Youth Folk Ensemble, and has since carved out a reputation as one of the most talented and interesting instrumentalists in the contemporary folk scene.

Known for a playing style that is both historically grounded and fiercely innovative, Owen is perhaps best celebrated for his critically acclaimed duo project with guitarist Louis Campbell, where they stretch traditional forms into cinematic and experimental new territories.

Their work has been described as “A masterpiece… Englands finest and most innovative instrumental duo” in Songlines, ‘A fusing of the traditional and the futuristic that is quietly groundbreaking, and beguiling with it" in The Times, and The Arts Desk Album of the Year. We first met Owen when he performed for our Thames festival season. Since then he's led our monthly traditional English sessions for nearly two years now, providing a home for enthusiasts from across the country to come together and make music communally. We're a big fan of the idea of eroding the boundaries between performers and audiences, and these nights are perhaps the clearest articulation of that ambition - the whole bar comes alive with fiddles and accordions, and even listening becomes active participation. It's hard not to tap along.

Petra Palkavosc

poetry and performance

Originally from a rural village in Slovakia, Petra Pálkovácsová is now firmly London-based. She's a poet, artist, researcher, and the founder of the independent publishing house Femmesocial Press. A vibrant voice in the experimental writing scene, she's earned a shortlisting for the Streetcake Experimental Writing Prize and published her debut collection, Mythologies, in 2023.

Petra's work constantly pushes the boundaries of how words are experienced, taking her performances everywhere from the European Poetry Festival to the Estorick Collection of Modern Italian Art. She is deeply passionate about independent publishing and has lectured on contemporary poetry at universities across the UK.

At Theatreship, Petra brings her literary expertise and sharp eye for emerging talent to our curation team. In regular Femmesocial Press launch nights she platforms fresh, underrepresented voices and bridges the gap between spoken word and live performance. Petra ensures that the ship's hold is always brimming with bold, brilliant storytelling.

More info on her work can be found here.

Isaiah Hull and THE NARRATOR

poetry, writing, and performance

Isaiah is a poet, musician, and self-described "stand-up tragedian" whose work moves fluidly between experimental storytelling, music, and performance. First making waves with Manchester’s Young Identity collective, Isaiah has since shared stages with the likes of Kae Tempest, Saul Williams, and Skepta, and released his debut poetry collection, Nosebleeds, to critical acclaim.

Whether he is delivering visceral spoken word, recording with the London-based collective life is beautiful, or preparing for his debut musical release with Young Recordings, Isaiah’s live sets play brilliantly with the extreme dualities of laughing and crying on stage.

At Theatreship, Isaiah brings his fiercely original approach to language down into our hold. Alongside fellow poet, and artistic director of Life is Beautiful THE NARRATOR, they created THE INVISIBLE WORKSHOP — a creative writing series that encourages intuitive, non-traditional, and experimental approaches to storytelling. Isaiah and the NARRATOR continue to champion process, play, and collaborative possibility on the water.

Skydaddy

New music incubator

As a central pillar of the London art-rock scene, British-Lebanese songwriter and producer Rachid Fakhre works at the bleeding edge of the UK’s indie underground. Recording under the moniker Skydaddy, Rachid is a fierce community builder whose legendary "Works in Progress" nights serve as an incubator for some of the most interesting new music in London. His nights onboard have featured new work and stripped-back sets from legendary artists including Squid, Cameron Picton (black midi), Tyler Hyde and Luke Mark (Black Country, New Road), Ethan P Flynn, Frank Ocean-cosigned producer MT Hadley, and Jasper Llewellyn (caroline). Beyond his own critically acclaimed releases and his foundational work as half of the duo Spang Sisters, Rachid has an incredible eye for the next generation of London's underground talent.

Lileth Chinn

Folk song

Lileth is a singer and performer with a love for traditional folk song and dance, and a particular interest in community-building and sharing story through song. As a singer she's performed with Martin Carthy and recorded with Les Caravans.

Lileth is a critical part of the team, enmeshed in all things Theatreship. You can find her working behind the scenes producing events, behind the bar serving a pint, or in front of it, leading the fortnightly Songs on the Water night.

Songs on the Water is a hugely special night: a free event open to everyone to share and enjoy traditional folk songs. The evening is an ode to the oral tradition of sharing stories and histories - the reflection, sorrow and laughter brought to life through shanties, ballads, chorus, protest song and more. There's something powerfully bonding in singing together as a group, and, more than almost any other event onboard, it's the instantiation of our aim to bring people together. You can read more about Lileth, and the Songs on the Water night in this lovely National Geographic feature.

Karl Lutchmayer

Classical music

Karl is a world-renowned concert pianist and a masterful storyteller. Celebrated both for his virtuosic playing and for his ability to bring classical music to life, Karl has spent his career breaking down the traditional barriers of the recital hall. As a professor at Trinity Laban Conservatoire and a regular broadcaster, he is uniquely gifted at demystifying complex compositions and sharing the rich histories behind the scores.

Onboard he's pioneered his 'Conversational Concert' - an engaging and highly accessible format that invites audiences into an active dialogue about the pieces being performed.

His international career has seen him perform in concert halls around the world - work which has made him the first Steinway artist of Indian origin, and earned him the Pride of India medal. His current professional focus, as director of Musiquity, is on reforming the Indian music curriculum so that traditional Indian music can be taught and recognised alongside the western classical canon.

At Theatreship, Karl brings his exceptional talent and deep pedagogical knowledge to our classical music curation.

Campbell Baum

Folk music

Campbell is on a mission to keep traditional music alive, breathing, and fundamentally communal. While he tours the world playing bass and saxophone in celebrated indie band Sorry, he is also the driving force behind Broadside Hacks. Founded in 2020, the project has evolved into a folk collective, independent record label, and live music promoter that sits right at the beating heart of the modern British folk revival.

At Theatreship, Campbell’s deep passion for folk music and curatorial skill has found the perfect (floating) home. He curates and programmes our regular London folk club onboard, as well as being behind some of our favourite gigs onboard, from work by living legends like BJ Cole, Shirley Collins, and Martin Carthy, to performances from the new-breed Goblin Band and Brown Wimpenny. It's not for nothing that Campbell's programming was hailed by TimeOut as one of the best night outs in London.

Tristram Fane Saunders

Poetry

Tristram Fane Saunders is a poet, writer, and one of our favourite literary voices in the UK. For several years he held the post of Poetry Critic at The Telegraph, where he was a passionate champion for the artform.

He is the editor of The Little Review, an irreverent magazine about poetry (whose launch events we have been delighted to host onboard). His books include the Seamus Heaney Prize-shortlisted Before We Go Any Further and (as editor) Edna St Vincent Millay: Poems & Satires. He is currently Fellow Commoner in the Creative Arts at Trinity College, Cambridge. Tristram brings a deep love of language, and his characteristic wit, to everything he does. His nights onboard have been no exception and have featured some of the most urgent voices in contemporary British poetry, including Fran Lock, Hannah Silva, and Joelle Taylor.

Vessel

The Ship then


Built in 1913, the ship was one of the sailing cargo barges that once filled London's docks. Transporting bulk dry cargoes like grain and coal from europe right into the heart of London.


Once a common sight in London's docks, the rise of containerisation in the 1960s moved shipping out of central London and lead to the eventual dereliction of the old docks.

With a riveted steel hull, and classic sweeping lines, the ship speaks to a different age of shipping.

The Ship now


As shipping evolved, so did the ship. With life-extending modifications to her hull and superstructure, she spent over 110 years in continuous service, trading bulk dry cargo around the port cities of Northern Europe.

Now, returning to West India Dock and converted to a performing arts theatre, she continues to serve the needs of the Docklands communities, as she always has.

The Ship in Canary Wharf


The ship has become a new public space, reflecting the rich history of the docks, while looking forwards to a future of live events and lively community.

The ship features a spacious exhibition space on the upper deck, a characterful community café bar on the lower deck, and a 110 seat theatre & cinema space in the old cargo hold. We can't wait to invite you onboard.

Location

Location


The Ship is moored on the Millwall Cutting, London. This is a convenient location, just 30 second walk from the South Quay DLR and a 5 minutes walk from Canary Wharf Jubilee line.

Volunteer

Get your hands dirty!


The Theatreship project is built on skilled and motivated people coming together and learning from each other. So naturally, skill-sharing is at the heart of what we do. We believe strongly that there should be no barrier to learning new skills, and that no existing skill set or ability prevents you from learning new, unfamiliar ones.

This is why we are always looking for volunteers to get involved with us. Maybe you have particular skills you think could contribute, such as in theatre, production, construction or boat maintenance? Maybe you just want to learn something completely new? We’d love to hear from you!

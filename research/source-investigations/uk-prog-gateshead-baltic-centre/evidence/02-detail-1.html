For Our Communities

Baltic’s programme for communities asks how can we be a good neighbour to the people around us? 

We collaborate with organisations and individuals based in Gateshead and further afield, working together on creative projects which respond to the ideas and concerns of people and their neighbourhoods.

Our work onsite at Baltic centres around creating space for encounters where everybody feels welcome and facilitating social connections between people to build understanding and solidarity. 

We also offer a range of activities and longer-term projects in community settings with an ethos of mutual learning, exchange and care. We aim to find points of connection with people and groups, working collaboratively with artists and community members on creative projects that have shared purpose.

 

Baltic is a Gallery of Sanctuary

Neighbourly works in collaboration with a long-standing and much valued local network of refugee support organisations and is informed by the Neighbourly Working Group, a group of people with lived experience of the UK asylum system, who meet every 2-3 months at Baltic and act as critical friends. Input from this group informs and guides our Neighbourly activities. 

In early 2022 we became the first Gallery of Sanctuary in England, awarded by City of Sanctuary UK. City of Sanctuary Awards recognise and celebrate organisations that go above and beyond to welcome people seeking sanctuary. 

As a Gallery of Sanctuary, we are part of a movement of welcome, embedding solidarity and compassion in our work to move towards a more welcoming UK for people rebuilding their lives here.

We achieve this in the following ways:

  • Using our artistic platforms to communicate a positive vision of welcome, and raise awareness about migration and displacement, including exhibition We all came here from somewhere and To Own Nothing But The Whole World
  • Creating purposeful spaces (offering support in resettling journeys) social spaces (facilitating social connections, opportunities for relationships of friendship and solidarity, and building empathy) and creative spaces (exploring trans-cultural ways of making, learning, eating and connecting.)
  • Working closely with people who have lived experience of the UK asylum system, providing spaces and opportunities for people with lived refugee and asylum-seeking experience to lead, as well as to participate in arts and culture.
  • Embedding inclusive and welcoming practices across our organisation, through an ongoing programme of staff training and learning.
  • Connecting and working closely with partners across different sectors of public action.

Join the Sanctuary and Culture Network

Make the North East a More Welcoming Place Through Arts and Culture

Are you passionate about building a more inclusive, welcoming region for people with lived experience of displacement? Whether you’re an artist, cultural worker, academic, refugee-led organisation, volunteer, or simply someone who cares about social justice – you are warmly invited to join the Sanctuary and Culture Network.

The network brings together people and organisations across the North East to share knowledge, strengthen access to the arts, and create sustainable opportunities for people seeking sanctuary. We believe culture has the power to connect us, and that everyone should have access to creative spaces where they feel seen, safe, and heard.

Together, we:

  • Share ideas and resources through regular meetings
  • Identify areas of need, opportunity, and collaboration
  • Work collectively to build a vibrant cultural offer that includes and supports people resettling or temporarily living in our region
     

Now in its fourth year, the network is currently coordinated by D6: Culture in Transit and Baltic Centre for Contemporary Art. Along with Baltic and D6, North East Museums was a founding member. We meet quarterly throughout the year at different cultural venues and community spaces. Meetings include practical workshops, discussion sessions, and informal opportunities to connect. We also keep in touch via a Google group.

The network is not formally constituted but aims to provide a mutually-supportive space to come together to take collective action.

Everyone is welcome. Whether you’re new to this work or already active in the sector, we would love for you to be part of shaping a more equitable cultural future in the North East.

Interested in joining or finding out more? Contact annieb@balticmill.com. 

Let’s come together to ensure that the North East is not just a place of sanctuary, but a place where everyone can thrive through culture and creativity.

Neighbourly

A long-term focus for Baltic’s communities programme centres around providing welcome and support for people with experience of migration and displacement. Known as Neighbourly, this programme includes, but is not limited to, bringing people together to celebrate cultural and faith events, facilitating spaces for shared conversation, making and food, language learning support, wellbeing and skills development. 

Neighbourly programmes and events include:

Language Cafe

Language Cafe is a free, fun, friendly place to meet new people and informally practice English speaking and listening. Language Cafe is monthly, on the first Saturday of each month from 14:30-17:00. It is free, you don’t need to book, just turn up. 

Free tea, coffee, soft drinks and snacks will be provided. Sessions are family friendly and include artist-led creative sessions for adults and children to keep us busy while we chat. We will also have special guests to meet and chat with to cover topics like community resources that might be useful, skills, opportunities and employability.

We welcome and encourage participation from people with refugee and asylum-seeking backgrounds as well as confident and native English speakers.

SkillsMatch Volunteering 

Delivered in partnership with the West End Refugee Service, SkillsMatch at Baltic offers a variety of volunteering opportunities for people seeking sanctuary in the North East. 

Volunteers for Baltic roles are recruited through the West End Refugee Service SkillsMatch portal. These are supported by the West End Refugee Service team to ensure that they speak to the skills, experiences and interests of asylum seekers and refugees, and offer valuable stepping-stones to rebuilding professional pursuits, building confidence, improving English and establishing friendships and networks.

ESOL Visits 

ESOL Visits at Baltic are free, last around a half day and include: 

  • A guided tour (choose from our Exhibition Spotlight Tour, Behind the Scenes Tour or a Quayside Tour exploring the local sights)
  • An artist-led workshop – inspired by Baltic’s current exhibitions, or any topics/interests of the group

You don’t strictly have to be an ESOL group to visit. Any group seeking sanctuary in the North East is welcome. 

ESOL group leaders can find out more information, or book a visit by e-mailing Annie Bedford on e-mail annieb@balticmill.com or calling 0191 440 4919.

International Art Club

International Art Club is a weekly after-school workshop, delivered in term blocks at schools in Gateshead.  It brings together children from sanctuary-seeking families with school friends in the wider community. The club aims to build empathy and intercultural awareness, using art to explore ideas around culture and identity. 

Celebrations and Special Events

We collaborate with organisations and individuals to co-create cultural and faith-based events which bring people together in peaceful and purposeful ways, creating space for community togetherness, sharing and mutual learning. 

Ongoing events include:

Open Iftar

Open Iftar is organised in partnership with Ramadan Tent Project and hosted at Baltic annually since 2019. Open Iftar is a celebration of unity and community, inviting people of all faiths and none to gather and break their fast during Ramadan. We have welcomed and fed over 2,000 people over 6 years.

Holi Festival of Colours

Holi, a Hindu festival celebrating the arrival of spring is organised in partnership with Gem Arts. With a shared belief in the power of the arts to enrich the lives of individuals and communities through celebrating our shared cultural diversity, and a commitment to creating inclusive spaces for people to come together, Baltic and GemArts have welcomed over 20,000 to Holi since its inception in 2021.

One-off Projects & Commissions

Alongside our ongoing engagement strands, which aim to provide regular and consistent support for individuals, and opportunities for community gathering, we often work responsively on one-off projects and commissions. These projects provide opportunities to work in partnership on a particular focus, often revolving around a question, social issue or creative idea emerging from conversations with people and partners. Projects have included the Murals Project with West End Refugee Service, Refugee Week activities and Counterpoints Arts.

West End Refugee Service Murals Project

Over the Autumn of 2021, we worked with the West End Refugee Service and artists from forced migration communities to create a series of colourful panels for their community garden. Working with print-maker Theresa Easton, the artists planned, designed, painted and installed the panels – a collaborative effort to bring vibrancy and a message of hope, welcome and togetherness to the garden.

The panels feature hearts, representing compassion for people fleeing war and persecution, bridges, to convey a sense of connection and togetherness across borders, and birds, to reflect journeys and migration. Collectively the panels are designed to brighten up the WERS garden; celebrate the skills, talents and contributions of artists with forced migration experience; provide a talking point in the garden for different groups; and convey a sense of hope and kindness to the surrounding community.

We installed the panels on a sunny day in January 2022, with food provided by the Samosa Sisters, a not for profit women’s organisation who support and empower vulnerable refugee women in the North-East, and footage was captured by filmmaker Tariq Ziad. Ideas for the murals came from a wide group of people at West End Refugee Service, and the final pieces were painted by Solomon Gebreyonas, Sadia Sikandar, Victoria Simanovski and Tran Bao-Long.

Hannah Barnes, Director of West End Refugee Service: “Thank you so much for the brilliant day today and for the beautiful mural panels! The work you have done with people has obviously made them feel part of something valuable and special, and we all love the huge difference the panels have made to the way the building feels on the outside!! It was so lovely to see everyone with their artworks today and talking about what they meant to them.”

Art Lab

Art Lab is an ongoing programme, launched in May 2019. It focuses on equitable access to high quality creative provision and encourages sustainable creative art, food and play practices for families. 

Families come together during school holidays to make art from recycled, found and natural materials with Art Lab artist in residence, Isabella Carreras. Activities focus on the environment and natural world, imagining sustainable futures and supporting youth voices for climate change advocacy.

Alongside this and core to Art Lab is the idea that food insecurity is a social and environmental justice issue, and that creativity has a role to play in combatting it.

We work with partners to cook together, grow together, eat together, try new things, and share creative approaches to reduce food waste. 

Activities are steered by the families who participate and by children in particular. Future activities are picked based on the feedback and ideas of the families who took part previously. 

As Art Lab is about equitable access to creative activity and food provision during the school holidays, we take a nuanced approach to who may find it useful to attend. Although we encourage families whose children usually access Free School Meals (FSM) to get involved we know that this is not a perfect metric and we encourage wider participation.

Art Lab was previously hosted specifically in Birtley but has recently moved to be hosted in Baltic to widen participation across local wards.

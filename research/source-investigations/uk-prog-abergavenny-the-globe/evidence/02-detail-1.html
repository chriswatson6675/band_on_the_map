The Classic Blues Show

Celebrating the music of BB King, Clapton, Hendrix, Muddy Waters, Peter Green and many more
Spend an evening in the company of this fine six-piece band to celebrate the British Blues explosion of the 1960s. From Muddy Waters to John Mayall and Peter Green, The Classic Blues Show recaptures the authentic sound of British Blues musicians and the artists who inspired them.

Emily Portman & Friends

Doors 18:00
Emily Portman is an award-winning singer and songwriter with a passion for traditional songs, hailed as “one of the new British folk scene’s most beguiling presences” (Uncut). She has toured widely for over 15 years as a bandleader, with The Furrow Collective, the folk chart-topping Sea Song Sessions, and in a duo with Leveret’s Rob Harbron. Emily was awarded a BBC Folk Award for Best Original Song and has released three critically acclaimed albums of original material that recast old ballads and fairytales into new shapes.
In 2026, Emily returns to the road with an inspired new live show previewing songs from her forthcoming solo album, Dominion of Spells, released in May 2026, alongside selected material from across her acclaimed catalogue.
Dominion of Spells marks a bold new chapter in Emily’s songwriting. Drawing on ballads and folktales, the album weaves lived experience with rich mythic imagery, charting a journey through dark woods at midlife in search of transformation and wonder. Shape-shifting selkies, fairy queens, foxes, and owl goddesses thread through contemporary story-songs exploring parenthood, home, burnout, grief, and the transformative power of creativity.
The new songs are brought vividly to life with a fresh live line-up featuring sought-after guitarist Louis Campbell and acclaimed cellist Lucy Revis (Before Breakfast). Together they create a richly layered yet understated sound world, with Emily’s luminous vocals, banjo, and piano at the centre, woven through crystalline harmonies, lush strings, and interweaving electric and acoustic guitars.
Audiences can expect an intimate, spellbinding performance where traditional influences meet contemporary storytelling. This preview tour offers an early glimpse into Dominion of Spells, an evening of vivid, songs steeped in mythic imagination.

Hay Talks: Connected, But At What Cost?

19:30 | free entry

Does social media connect our communities, or damage them?

Social media has transformed the way we communicate, share information and build communities. It can bring people together across distance, give individuals and local groups a voice, help small businesses reach new audiences and create communities around shared interests.
But are we really more connected?
From online arguments and misinformation to social pressure, polarisation and the growing amount of time we spend behind screens, there are growing questions about what social media is doing to the communities we live in. Does having more ways to connect actually bring us closer together, or can our digital lives create new divisions and leave us feeling more disconnected from the people around us?
Join us at The Globe for a lively and accessible debate exploring both sides of the question. We'll bring together different perspectives from across our local community to consider how social media is changing the way we communicate, participate in public life and build relationships.
Is social media helping our communities thrive — or is it damaging the connections that hold them together?
Come along, hear different perspectives, and have your say. The debate will be followed by questions from the audience, so there will be plenty of opportunity to join the conversation.
Meet Our Panel:
  • Rich Matthews – Development Director at Rural Media, supporting rural young people and underserved communities through film, media and digital arts. Teaching Fellow and Visiting Lecturer at the University of Birmingham, researching digital behaviours and community.
  • Dr Giandomenico Di Domenico– Assistant Professor in Marketing and Strategy at Cardiff Business School, specialising in misinformation, digital trust and the psychological and social effects of online communication.
  • Dr Sarah Williams – Academic Tutor in Media and Communication Studies at Swansea University, specialising in digital and media literacy, digital citizenship, and the social and cultural impact of digital technologies.

Fungi Town Festival: A Cultural History Of The Magic Mushroom In Britian

15:30-16:30
In this conversation, Professor Andy Letcher of Exeter University will discuss the cultural history of the magic mushroom, with a focus on the UK. There is renewed interest in the potential of psychedelics to address a number of stubborn mental illnesses, as well as a burgeoning psychedelic underground, driven especially by people travelling to the Amazon to consume ayahuasca in healing ceremonies. In this talk Andy will challenge many of the myths that have arisen around magic mushrooms, answering questions such as: were the builders of Stonehenge tripping and was Santa Claus originally a mushroom munching shaman?

Fungi Town Festival: Rethinking Decay

10:00-11:30
Join Rich Wright, a conservation mycologist with over 20 years' experience in fungal ecology, rare species translocation and cultivation, for a talk on rethinking decay in Britain's oaks. Having held posts at Kew, Cardiff University and Plantlife, Rich brings deep scientific and field knowledge to heart-rot, a process often misunderstood despite its huge value for woodland biodiversity. The talk draws on years spent among ancient oaks and their fungi, exploring what decay can teach us about the living world, and ourselves.

Polly Paulusma & Dan Whitehouse: Songs Before Sunrise

7pm
Expect an intimate, deeply atmospheric evening: songs, stories, spoken word with two of the UK’s most distinctive and innovative songwriter–performers.
 Across the evening, Dan Whitehouse and Polly Paulusma create a shared musical space where song and spoken word blur, and where two distinct voices meet in compelling dialogue. Moving between solo moments and subtle interplay, the set flows from sparse piano and guitar-led songs into more expansive textures, with each artist responding to the other’s work in real time.
 Dan Whitehouse’s work has been described as “Intriguing” and “Gorgeous” **** (The Guardian), and “Unflinching… haunting” (Clash Magazine), marking him out as a songwriter of rare emotional clarity.
His most recent material, Only Love, shaped between England and Japan, explores presence, distance and fatherhood with striking honesty and his back catalogue has been celebrated by Folk On Foot and BBC Radio 6 for Dan’s insight into social history and the human condition.

Since her debut Scissors In My Pocket on One Little Independent Records, Polly Paulusma has built a critically acclaimed body of work spanning songwriting, production and academic research, and has toured internationally including supporting Bob Dylan and Coldplay. Her work has been widely praised, with The Guardian noting “a voice capable of some unexpected twists” and Rolling Stone describing her debut as “an enchanting… intelligent folk pop”. Her recent album Wildfires brings her evolving fusion of spoken word and song to full expression.
An evening shaped by process as much as performance. Both artists draw from the discipline known as ‘morning pages’ writing before the day begins, before the critical voice takes hold.

Lucy Farrell and Shane Pendergast

8pm

Rooted in the rich traditions of English and Maritime folk music, Lucy Farrell and Shane Pendergast bring together two distinct yet complementary voices in contemporary folk. A special one-off collaborative tour in the UK.

Lucy Farrell, a BBC Radio 2 Folk Award winner, is celebrated for her poetic songwriting, unconventional melodies, and the crystalline clarity of her voice. Known for her work with The Furrow Collective, Eliza Carthy and the Wayward Band, and a host of other acclaimed collaborations, Farrell has carved out a singular place in English folk with her elegant, understated tenor guitar and viola arrangements. Her solo work reveals a deep lyricism and a rare ability to make the strange feel familiar, and the familiar feel extraordinary.
 
Shane Pendergast, hailing from Prince Edward Island, Canada, carries forward a long lineage of Maritime storytellers and songkeepers. A troubadour of the East Coast, his plainspoken yet profound songwriting has earned him recognition at folk festivals worldwide, as well as Contemporary Roots Album of the Year at the Music PEI Awards for Second Wind (2021). His 2025 release Winter Grace offers a deeply personal reflection on the joys and hardships of life in the Maritimes.

Hannah James and Toby Kuhn

8PM
Innovative folk artist Hannah James reunites with dynamic cellist Toby Kuhn for a series of live shows celebrating their new album, Where All Our Names Belong. Together they weave a rich tapestry of song, story, and rhythm - Hannah's expressive voice, accordion, and percussive dance entwined with Toby's vivid, unorthodox cello delivery.
Hannah is an award-winning musician, dancer, songwriter and singer, a creative force renowned for her work in Lady Maisery and collaborations with Seasick Steve, Maddy Prior and Songs of Separation with Eliza Carthy, Karine Polwart and others. Toby is an award-winning French cellist whose post-classical, inventive, improvisational playing has taken him all across the globe. 
Their performances unfold as an immersive journey: soulful songs, playful dances, and unexpected textures that shift effortlessly between delicacy and power. Ever curious, ever inventive, Hannah and Toby blur the lines between tradition and innovation, inviting audiences into moments of connection, discovery, and joy.

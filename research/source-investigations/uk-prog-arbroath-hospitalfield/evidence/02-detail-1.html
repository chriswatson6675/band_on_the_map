Introducing the artists selected for the Autumn Residency

Hospitalfield is delighted to introduce the selected Artists that have joined us this week for the Autumn Residency Programme.

The Autumn Residency offers 6 UK based artists the opportunity to spend four weeks on residency with the support of a bursary as well as studio visits from our guest mentors. This year the mentors are Curator Laura Clarke, and Artist Caspar Heinemann.

As well as our Autumn funded residents we are very excited to welcome Beñat Krolem who joins us as part of a reciprocal exchange residency programme with Tabakalera, International Centre of Contemporary Culture in Donostia/San Sebastián, Basque Autonomous Community, Spain. Through this exchange Beñat  joins us here at Hospitalfield for a two week period before heading to Glasgow Sculpture Studios  for a further two weeks. The reciprocal Scottish artist will be hosted at Tabakalera mid-February

The Autumn Residents are:

 

Philip Ewe’s practice encompasses live performances, interventions, photography, film and installation.  He crafts work often in public (and not-so-public) spaces, channeling the rapidly redeveloping city and its attendant mental states, exploring the medieval figure of the fool and – through improvisation and scripting – the psychological space between the city and the individual as sites of Capital and speculation.

Within his live performances he draw on the process of socialization—the voices and messages involved in the internalization of social norms and ideologies.  Coming from a proud LGBTQI history of subverting urban space, researching locations, as stages and inspiration, is central to my practice.    His wider practice of interventions and actions includes shifting earth from outside a gallery into it, moving his bedroom from London to Scotland, or replicating my immigrant grandparents’ fish & chip as miniature model.

Selected UK presentations include: The Royal Academy of Art, South London Gallery, Whitechapel Gallery, Cubitt, London; Strange Field, Glasgow Women’s Library, Scotland

Richard Porter (b. 1988, UK) is an artist working across sculpture, painting, drawing, collage and performance. His elemental practice is a direct conversation with the void and the unknown, striving to configure a queer space that is open and free from conventional constrictions.

His solo exhibitions ‘2’ (2026) and Night Glyph (2021) were held at Amanda Wilkinson, London. He has participated in group exhibitions including Origin Story at Gordon Robichaux, New York (2023), After That Blue at Fragment, New York (2023), Derek Jarman’s Modern Nature at John Hansard Gallery, Southampton (2022), and I Know Where I’m Going, Who Can I Be Now at The Modern Institute, Glasgow (2021).

In addition to his own practice, he runs the queer independent publisher, Pilot Press.

Harmeet Singh Rahal (b. 1996, Mumbai) is a London-based artist, working across moving-image, print, text, sound and installation. His practice is an inquiry into the poetics of rebellion, bootlegged histories, and fabulations of criminality within the police state.
His work has been shown at Bloomberg New Contemporaries (Camden Art Centre and Grundy Art Gallery), Next/Now (Vadehra Art Gallery, New Delhi), Contraband Zindabad (Flatland Projects, Bexhill-On-Sea), Peoples Freedom 75 (Bhupesh Gupta Bhavan, Mumbai), The Selected 14 UK Tour, and CIRCA Prize screenings in London, Berlin, and Milan.

Rahal received the Almacantar Award for his Goldsmiths degree show, the British Council Venice Biennale Fellowship, the FLAMIN Fellowship, and the Flatland Solo Exhibition Award, and was featured in Art India’s 30 artists under 30. In 2025, he received the Deptford X Supported Commission, the Elephant Trust grant, and Arts Council England’s DYCP award.

Suzanna Slack is a writer and artist experimenting with memory, childhood, care and the archive.  Diaries, scrapbooks, journals, collage, photo albums and anything deemed frivolous, messy, sentimental and childish.  Their work is fixated on documentation and rumination, haunting and being-haunted.  Vulnerable bodies creating bodies of vulnerable work on paper, torn and tattered, detective work against AI, a nightmare to store and to some just a toxic hoard.  But perhaps a non toxic hoard against the hoard of data we’re trying to normalise?  Who knows?  They are currently recreating a cave of testimony, a little maze of true-life doomed romance and codependent femme and single parent self-education.  They took their first painting classes at Citylit in the autumn of 2025.

Linda Stupart is an artist, writer, and educator from Cape Town, South Africa currently living in Birmingham, UK. Their work thinks through climate change, embodiment, abjection and the transgression of borders.

Stupart’s practice is generally driven by a radical site-specificity; immersing themselves in the ecologies; histories, and fictions of the immediate environment, while unearthing and troubling straight(forward) narratives of place. Stupart’s work consists predominantly of performance, film, writing and sculpture. Their first novella, Virus, was published in 2016, and translated into Spanish in 2023.

Stupart worked in arts universities for 15 years before retraining as a horse-riding instructor and equine assisted learning facilitator. Stupart also runs South Birmingham Studios, a co-operative studio space in Birmingham and has a long-running collaborative practice with Carle Gent.

 

Zoe Williams is a London-based artist working across sculpture, installation, performance, drawing and scenography. Her work explores the fetishistic nature of materials and their relationship to embodied forms of consumption and expenditure. Through her work, she wishes to generate conversations around the pressures of power, the politics of sex and the economics of production.

She is represented by the Ciaccia Levi Gallery, Paris-Milan, and has worked with a range of international institutions including: Fraeme Projects, La Friche la Belle de Mai, Marseille; Somerset House, London; Tate St Ives; Roberts Institute of Art, London; DCA, Dundee; Spike Island, Bristol; Dortmunder Kunstverein; Heidelberger Kunstverein; Mimosa House, London; Le Crédac, Ivry-Sur-Seine and Villa Arson, Nice. Her work is part of the FRAC Nouvelle-Aquitaine La Méca collection in France, Collezione Fondazione Sandretto Re Rebaudengo, Turin, Italy, and CAPC Musée d’art Contemporain de Bordeaux Collection.

 

Beñat Krolem (Bilbao, 1990) studied at the University of the Basque Country (EHU) and at the University of the West of England (UWE Bristol). His work explores sound, oral, and visual improvisation, focusing on the performative dimension of the body and its relationship with space. Through sculpture and the construction of devices, displays and furniture, he is interested in improvisational movements and the communities that sustain them, paying special attention to the gestures and materials that emerge through making, as well as to situations in which encounter plays a central role.

He was a resident at the Solomon R. Guggenheim Museum (New York) as part of the Basque Artists Program and recent exhibitions and performances have taken place at La Casa Encendida (Madrid), Azkuna Zentroa, Guggenheim Museum Bilbao and Sala Rekalde (Bilbao), Kursaal (Donostia/San Sebastián), Studio D (London), and Fondazione 107 and the Accademia Albertina di Belle Arti (Turin).

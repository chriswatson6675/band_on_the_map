Hamlet

Wed 10 Mar - Sat 13 Mar 2027
Production Company:
Rhinoceros Theatre Company
BOOK TICKETS

Rhinoceros Theatre Company brings Shakespeare's Hamlet to Hampton Hill Theatre this March, directed by Nigel Andrews.

A prince torn between his father's ghost and his own soul. A court built on murder and masquerade. A play within a play that exposes the truth. Shakespeare's greatest tragedy, told in full - with all its wit, its rage, and its ghosts.

Performances:

Wednesday 10 March - 7.30pm
Thursday 11 March - 7.30pm
Friday 12 March - 7.30pm
Saturday 13 March - 1.30pm (matinee) and 6.30pm

Running time: approx. 3 hours, including a 20 minute interval

School Offer:
A live, professional-standard staging of a core GCSE/A-level text - ghost, play-within-a-play, swordfight and all. Director Nigel Andrews leans into the play's "under-drama" - the ghosts, dreams and hidden truths beneath the famous lines - for a genuinely fresh angle on the text. A "Meet the Director" Q&A can be arranged before a performance: [email protected]. £15 per student for groups of 10+ (standard price £26).

A note from the Director:
The play's the thing - but what lies beneath it?
It's a thrill for us at Rhinoceros Theatre - and an inspiring challenge - to present the greatest play by the world's greatest playwright. Shakespeare's Hamlet is not just a drama with the greatest language ever heard on stage; count the famous quotes you never knew came from one play. It's an enthralling tale of murder and misdeed, and the exciting, profound, brilliantly told story of a young man struggling to transcend his own tragedy, to amend or avenge injustice, and to grow up into a man in charge of his own life, soul and destiny.

Fascinatingly and uniquely, it's also a Shakespeare play about plays and playing - about how these shape us and how, sometimes, we can shape them. In Hamlet, a man guilty of murder is exposed by a play within a play. Hamlet, the prince who exposes him, is himself an actor of a kind: a performative prodigy of changing moods and quicksilver thoughts, of masquerades and guises, of wit, guile and a gift of the gab unsurpassed by any other Shakespeare character. "Speak the speech trippingly," he famously instructs the travelling players who present his play. And they do. And so, we dare hope, will our own cast.

Yet what lies beneath the spoken words in Hamlet? What world lurks behind the famous soliloquies and the rapier dialogue? Few productions do justice, in my view, to what might be called the invisible dimension of Hamlet - the under-drama. This, after all, is a play about dreams and visions, about deep-hidden dimensions of the human soul, about ghosts and spectres. Of course, we do meet one of them: the ghost of Hamlet's father. It's a play about unearthly visitations, about mortal and immortal intimations, about the fears that stalk us all, by night or day.

So expect a production that delves beneath the play's lines to find what I'd call the play's "mines" - a recurring word in Hamlet. Those explosive truths, revelations, nightmares and awakenings to which we are all heir, or prey, as human beings living in this world.

Nigel Andrews
Director, Hamlet

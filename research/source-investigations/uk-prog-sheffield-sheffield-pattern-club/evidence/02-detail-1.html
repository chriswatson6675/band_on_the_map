Event Details

  • Venue: DINA, Fitzalan Square

  • Time: 6pm-8pm 24th Jan 2023

  • Sign up here – free!

Blackwork was a popular 16th and early 17th century embroidery technique, characterised by its use of black silk thread (though other colours are sometimes seen) worked on white linen. Over the roughly 100 years of its application Blackwork Embroidery evolved significantly from the early reversible counted-thread style through to the geometric infill patterns and shaded style worked in ‘speckle’ or ‘seed’ stitch.

Contemporary Blackwork primarily uses simple straight stitches across the regular grid structure of evenweave fabric to create repeating geometric patterns.

In this workshop Toni Buckby will lead us in exploring how these patterns are formed and look at some of the more complex plaited braid stitches used in the historic technique. Materials supplied, no experience of needlework is required!

Kolam is a heritage folk art from Tamil Nadu in Southern India. These are hand-drawn patterns made on a grid of dots (pulli) and are passed down through generations of Tamil women. The Kolam tradition is millennia old, diffused between regions (termed Rangoli in the North, Muggu in Telugu-speaking regions), continually evolving, and connected to ritual, aesthetics, and religion.

In this workshop we’ll be introduced to a method for drawing Kolam by Anuradha Reddy, following templates generated live for us by a hacked receipt printer.

No specialised equipment or prior knowledge needed, but feel free to bring a favourite pencil and eraser if you like, otherwise we’ll have plenty for you to borrow.

If you’re curious you can watch Anuradha talk about Kambi Kolam, or read a related text.

08sep15:0018:00Performing with patterns: live coding workshop with Yaxu + Eye Measure

Event Details

We’re doing a three-hour dive into live coding as part of the Sheffield Showcase weekend! Date: Sun 8

Event Details

We’re doing a three-hour dive into live coding as part of the Sheffield Showcase weekend!

Date: Sun 8 Sep 2024 3:00 PM – 6:00 PM
Location: Soft Ground, The Moor, Sheffield S1 4PF
Tickets £0 – £25: https://www.tickettailor.com/events/patternclub/1366388?

Live coding is where people make music by executing code in real-time, usually projecting that code so the audience can see it too. 

The Strudel platform for live coding musical patterns is an accessible version of TidalCycles for the web, available to use for free on desktop or tablet. It is straightforward to use and requires very little advanced knowledge – if you’re happy messing around, trying things out and seeing what it sounds like, you can make great sounding music very quickly!

In this workshop, we’ll explore approaches to performing music live using Strudel. We will show you how to construct and change different patterns in real time; use your own samples in your music and use transitions to support pace and tension within live performances.

There will be short walk-throughs of performances from us (Yaxu and Eye Measure) to explain our workflows, and the opportunity for you to create your own music, either alone or collaboratively within the group.

This workshop is open to beginners and those with some experience alike. We will start with a quick introduction to Strudel and provide you with code templates, meaning you’ll be able to start making sounds within minutes even if you have never used it before and even if you have no musical knowledge. For those with some live coding familiarity, we hope this workshop will provide new inspiration for performing and give you some new ideas!

Participants should bring their own laptop/tablet and headphones. If you can’t do this let us know as soon as possible and we will try to source one for you, but can’t promise anything

Cost: Free, but pre-booking essential – tickets available here

Join dancer, choreographer, researcher, and educator Janani Suresh Ram for a two-hour introductory workshop that brings together the classical Indian dance form Bharatanatyam and the world of mathematical patterns.

This immersive session weaves history, performance, visual media, and embodied exploration. Participants will be introduced to the aesthetic and technical foundations of Bharatanatyam while discovering how mathematical ideas—such as infinity and fractals—can be experienced through movement.

No prior experience in dance or mathematics is required. The workshop is designed to be accessible, engaging, and experiential.

Participation is free.
A free ticket is required as spaces are limited. If you are unable to attend after registering, please let us know so your spot can be offered to someone else.

Workshop Structure

Part 1 | Foundations (30 minutes)

  • Introduction to the history of Bharatanatyam

  • Overview of Bharatanatyam technique

  • Live performance of traditional pieces

Part 2 | Math & Movement (30 minutes)

  • Illustrated presentation on intersections of mathematics and Bharatanatyam
    (including concepts such as infinity and fractals, using slides and short films)

  • Introduction to Janani’s research and book

  • Reflection and Q&A

Part 3 | Embodied Exploration (60 minutes)

  • Hands-on movement workshop exploring mathematical ideas through dance

Event Details

Where: Pattern Club alpacalab, 2-4 Matilda St (just off the moor, above EE), Sheffield S1 4QD
When: 6:30pm – 8:30pm, Tuesday 14th April 2026
Tickets: free signup required
Who: People curious about making strange things with patterns

What: Our monthly Sheffield pattern club meetup. All people interested in the stranger side of patterns welcome! These meetups will be focussed on sharing practice-lead investigations into pattern-making in heritage and contemporary arts, crafts and music. For example if you’re a textile/fibre artist who is curious about creative coding, a coder who wants to be a musician, a musician who is curious about choreography, or a choreographer who wants to integrate braiding into their work, an origami artist who.. well you get the idea! We want to make this a safe, fun and diverse space to share ideas. We actively encourage ‘imposters’ to attend!

This time we’ll be looking at the structure of woven patterns, in two ways. Firstly we’ll try out “reverse drafting”, where we take small pieces of woven fabric and take it apart to try to understand how the interlacements work. This can be a very surprising experience that might change how you look at fabric! We will have enough fabric for everyone, but feel free to also bring along interesting-looking woven fabric you don’t mind taking apart in order to work out how it works.

We’ll also have a look at the free/open source AdaCAD web-based weaving software (bring a laptop if you have one handy!) to try out our understanding of the weaves and look at more complex patterning techniques. 

If we have time we will also play with weaving paper strips to get further hands-on understanding, and have a look at the pattern club 8-shaft table loom.

Unfortunately our current studio doesn’t have step-free access. Please get in contact if you have problems with this or other access requirements and we’ll try our best to accommodate you.

Event Details

Where: Pattern Club alpacalab, 2-4 Matilda St (just off the moor, above EE), Sheffield S1 4QD
When: 6:30pm – 9pm, Monday 14th Sep 2026
Tickets: free signup required
Who: People curious about making strange things with patterns

What: Our (roughly) monthly Sheffield pattern club meetup. All people interested in the stranger side of patterns welcome! These meetups will be focussed on sharing practice-lead investigations into pattern-making in heritage and contemporary arts, crafts and music. For example if you’re a textile/fibre artist who is curious about creative coding, a coder who wants to be a musician, a musician who is curious about choreography, or a choreographer who wants to integrate braiding into their work, an origami artist who.. well you get the idea! We want to make this a safe, fun and diverse space to share ideas. We actively encourage ‘imposters’ to attend!

This time textile boffin Laura Devendorf will be visiting from the US in September, dropping by Sheffield for this workshop. Laura is an amazing experimental weaver and creative technologist, and runs the unstable design lab in Boulder Colorado. She made the awesome free/open source AdaCAD software for playful, algorithmic design of weave structures. In this beginner-friendly workshop, we’ll be getting into the structure of woven patterns, by working with paper strips, threads, table looms and AdaCAD.

This workshop is well-suited both for total beginners to weaving, and more experienced weavers who are interested in the possibilities of designing weaves with AdaCAD.

Unfortunately our current studio doesn’t have step-free access. Please get in contact if you have problems with this or other access requirements and we’ll try our best to accommodate you.

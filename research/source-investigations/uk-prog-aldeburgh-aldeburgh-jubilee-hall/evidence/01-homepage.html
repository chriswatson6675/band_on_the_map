Join ‘Suffolk boys’ Mike Warner and Richard Negus in a captivating conversation about custodianship of our countryside and coast.
Richard as a hedgelayer, and Mike as a fisherman, share many years experience of working ‘in the field’ and ‘out at sea’. They now also champion farming and fishing, which are such vital allies to our rural and coastal communities, landscape and wildlife.
Mike & Richard discuss the realities, challenges and successes of working the land and sea. As well as the future opportunities that can be harnessed to sustain a thriving countryside and coast.

Mike Warner – A Passion for Seafood 
Mike is a born and bred Suffolk food writer, specialising in telling the ‘Net to Plate’ story of how our seasonal and regional British catch is caught and landed, whilst connecting producer to consumer. Mike is passionate about the sustainability of not just our native fish stocks, but the fishing communities themselves; their tradition, culture and heritage. He is also an independent seafood and fisheries consultant, working with mainly hospitality and media clients to help forge the traceability connection and better understanding of the realities of seafood production.
He is a member of the Guild of Food Writers, a trustee of the fishermen’s charity, Fishing into the Future and chairman of the Shellfish Association of Great Britain.
@apassionforseafood

Richard Negus – Words from the Hedge
Richard is a former soldier and professional horseman. He now lays hedges for a living and is recognised for leading hedgerow management and rejuvenation. Richard is a practical conservationist, writer and now author of the widely acclaimed Words from the Hedge – A Hedgelayer’s View of the Countryside.
Richard co-hosts a weekly podcast, Countryslide, covering British rural life, and his writing features in The Daily Telegraph, The Critic and Scribehound. He is Suffolk born and lives in the heart of his beloved county.
@troopersnooks

22nd December, 1917. There is a knock at the door of Millicent Fawcett’s home on Gower Street, Bloomsbury. Lady Ottoline Morrell has crossed from her home at 44 Bedford Square to express her condolences for the loss of a beloved sister.

Millicent Fawcett, leader of the suffragists, is about to open the door to the patroness of the Bloomsbury Group. Ottoline’s vast circle of friends includes Prime Ministers and novelists, painters and philosophers. Her world is tainted by the whiff of scandal and the whisper of notoriety. Millicent on the other hand has been unwavering in her dedication to the crusade for women’s suffrage.

Across The Square imagines what might have occurred in an encounter between these two sharply contrasting women from very different worlds. Each in their own way, Lady Ottoline Morrell and Millicent Fawcett have left an indelible legacy on our political and cultural landscape yet they have languished too long in the twilight of obscurity, overshadowed by others.

In a show full of both laughter and drama, Across the Square seeks to shine a light on these immensely influential women. Written by international award-winning writer, Non Vaughan-O’Hagan and directed by Alex Pearson, Across the Square had its premiere at the Bloomsbury Festival and a sellout revival at London’s Jermyn Street Theatre. Will these two extraordinary women find common ground? Two differing world views are about to collide over a glass of sherry.

Apollo Theatre Company presents Cult Figure: Kenneth Williams
Written and Performed by Colin Elmer
Directed by Tim Astley

Kenneth Williams was one of the most unique and beloved figures in British comedy history.  From show stealing performances on the radio in Hancock’s Half Hour and Round the Horne and over twenty years of starring in the Carry On films to becoming the darling of the chat shows, Williams was a rare talent who could turn from broad slapstick to erudite wit in a split second.

Celebrated actor and impressionist, Colin Elmer, who played Kenneth Williams in the UK tours of Round the Horne and Hancock’s Half Hour, reprises his role in this new production, telling the great man’s story in his own words, using anecdotes, writings and some of the material for which the comedy legend was so well-known.

So stop messing about and join us for a hilarious and engaging evening in the company of a true cult figure.

As the shipping forecast embarks on its second century, Charlie Connelly’s hilarious and informative show brings the quirky broadcast vividly to life, celebrating what he firmly believes to be the greatest invention of the modern age.
How did a weather forecast for mariners capture the hearts of a nation, from salty old sea dog to insomniac landlubber? How is it possible for ‘rain later’ to be ‘good’? And where the hell is North Utsire?

Delving into the history of the forecast and the extraordinary people who made it, Charlie explains what all those curious phrases really mean, celebrates its wide cultural impact, shares rip-roaring adventures from his own extraordinary journey through the 31 sea areas and presents his case for the shipping forecast being our finest achievement.

Inspired by Charlie’s bestselling book, which has sold more than a quarter of a million copies, this pacy, moving and hilarious show is selling out theatres up and down the land as it takes you all the way from Viking to South-East Iceland. Attention All Shipping guarantees the shipping forecast will never sound the same again.

Aldeburgh Comedy Weekend

Friday 16th – Sunday 18th October 2026

 

Following its triumphant launch in 2025, the Aldeburgh Comedy Weekend is back to bring you more star-studded joy and laughter. We have stand-up from Jack Dee, Ed Byrne, Kiri Pritchard-McLean and Mark Watson. The incomparable Jo Brand will be in conversation with Clive Anderson, while this year’s Lunchtime Achievement Award goes to John Lloyd, producer of Blackadder, Spitting Image and QI. Sofie Hagen, born in Denmark but relatable everywhere, is in our Have I Got New For You slot. Finally, there’s nothing like a dame, so don’t miss Dame Joanna Lumley choosing her favourite comedy film clips at the Aldeburgh Cinema.

Jack Dee: Jack’s Joke Show

Friday 16th October, 7pm

Jack’s dry humour and deadpan delivery have delighted us for thirty-five years. His tv career began in 1991 with Channel 4’s The Jack Dee Show and he’s gone on to feature in many acclaimed tv comedies, including the BBC2 sitcom Lead Balloon (which he co-wrote) and the hit ITV Show, Bad Move. In 2004, he was nominated for a BAFTA for Jack Dee Live At The Apollo. Five years later, he replaced Humphrey Lyttelton as chairman of the much-loved Radio 4 ‘antidote to panel games’, I’m Sorry I Haven’t A Clue, so he’s now well on his way to being a National Treasure. (He’s still too young.) He also hosted the hugely popular The Apprentice: You’re Hired, and The Apprentice: You’re Fired (BBC2). We’re thrilled we’ve hired him to launch our Comedy Weekend. In the first half, he’ll do stand-up, then after the interval he’ll give us a generous taster from his new show, Jack’s Joke Show. Jokes and funny anecdotes have intrigued him for as long as he can remember, so he’ll be sharing the influences that have shaped his comedy career. It’s a nostalgic treat he promotes with the unforgettable selling point: ‘Guaranteed no new material’.

Tickets £30 available HERE

 

Ed Byrne Live

Friday 16th October, 10pm

Ed’s many tv appearances include hosting Live At The Apollo, being a regular panellist on Mock The Week, QI and Have I Got News For You, and taking part in Celebrity Mastermind and Celebrity Pointless. Go to YouTube and watch him at the Melbourne International Festival (2024), and you’ll see immediately that he’s a master of observational comedy. The most famous comedian ever to have studied Horticulture at the University of Strathclyde, Ed’s been a guest on The Graham Norton Show six times, so he knows how to sit back and join in the laughter. Now it’s your turn. This is your chance to see a comedian more used to playing venues six times the size of the Aldeburgh Jubilee Hall. In the words of The Scotsman, ‘It’s rare for a comic to merit the word ‘masterful’ but in this case it is no overstatement’, while Edinburgh Evening News described him as, ‘A great comedian making the simple sublime’. Let’s leave the last word to The Times: ‘This is just glorious – full of gnarly little joy pockets’. Little? Ed’s joy pockets are big enough for all of us.

Sofie Hagen: I Think Some Of This Is My Fault 

Saturday 17th October, 12pm

Sofie’s new to Aldeburgh so we’ve created a special Have I Got New For You slot in her honour. She’s from Denmark but learned English at the age of four, ‘just so I could go to Aldeburgh in 2026 and do stand-up’. Back in 2015, she won Best Newcomer at the Edinburgh Festival Fringe, with her show Bubblewrap – which Brian Logan of The Guardian called ‘compelling and beautifully constructed’. Since then, she’s gone from strength to strength, appearing at Festivals all over the world, including Melbourne, Montreal, Johannesburg and Berlin, and on tv for Netflix, BBC, and Comedy Central. She’s also gained a great reputation as a podcaster. She’s the creator and host of the very successful Who Hurt You?  – intimate, and funny conversations about why we are the way we are, with guests including Aisling Bea and Katherine Ryan. Sofie’s always tried to do the right thing, but in this show she asks herself the big question: Did I do the right thing or not? Sofie’s funny, fresh, thoughtful and endearing. Don’t miss her.

Mark Watson: Before It Overtakes Us

Saturday 17th October, 3pm

Radio 4 favourite, novelist, quizzer, stand-up, comedy writer, Taskmaster cheeky text icon – Mark Watson is the nation’s favourite comedy polymath. Who else has performed shows on a ferry, from a stream, on a train, and in a vaccination queue? Or completed a Comedy Marathon by performing non-stop for twenty-four hours? His stint at the Comedy Weekend is a mere sprint but, as Chortle said, ‘An hour or two of our limited span on Earth in his uplifting, witty company is time excellently  spent.’ In Before It Overtakes Us, he’ll be pondering the uncertain future facing humanity, the worst word he’s ever said in public and – of course – the current state of the UK sausage industry. You’ll probably recognise Mark from Mock The Week, Celebrity Pointless, Have I Got News For You, Would I Lie to You?, Live At The Apollo and QI. His Radio 4 series are myriad – including Mark Watson Makes The World Substantially Better, in which he looked at the human condition through the prism of the Seven Deadly Sins  (and a few Virtues) and Mark Watson Talks A Bit About Life assisted by long-time associates Tim Key and Tom Basden.

Good Evening, Jo Brand

Hosted by Clive Anderson

Saturday 17th October, 7pm

Jo was a pioneer. Britain just didn’t have female stand-ups when she and Victoria Wood started out; and, unlike Victoria, Jo couldn’t get any respite by sitting down at the piano. Famously a psychiatric nurse before she became a comedian, her poise under pressure from hecklers and blokes was fabulous – she could deal with anyone, and she did. TV soon followed: Friday Night Live, Have I Got News For You, Would I Lie To You, a guest appearance on Absolutely Fabulous, a ghost (sic) appearance in A Christmas Carole, and nine series of Great British Bake-Off. She was also the star and co-writer of Getting On, the award-winning series set in a geriatric ward, for which she won a BAFTA, and the feature film The More You Ignore Me (based on her novel), which the Daily Telegraph called ‘an appealing bitter-sweetly funny tale’. In conversation with Clive Anderson, she’ll be celebrating forty years in comedy, and taking questions from you. Forty years of being humane and funny and coping with marriage, parenting, and ageing. As she herself says, in her inimitable way: ‘I’m happily married. Unfortunately he’s not’.

CLIVE ANDERSON

Clive Anderson deserves an Aldeburgh Loyalty Card. In 2023, he brought his one-man show Me, Macbeth & I to the Jubilee Hall, while at last year’s Comedy Weekend he interviewed Alison Steadman. Long-term host of Radio 4’s Loose Ends, his lightning wit and spontaneity led to many unforgettable encounters on his tv talk shows. (Just ask Jeffrey Archer, Barry Gibb and Cher.) Clive won a British Comedy Award as Best Entertainment Presenter for Whose Line Is It Anyway, and has crossed tv paths with Jo Brand many times, as fellow guests on Have I Got News For You and QI.

A Lunchtime Achievement Award: John Lloyd 

Hosted by Jon Canter

Sponsored by The Suffolk 

Sunday 18th October, 12pm

Legendary comedy producer John Lloyd has won more BAFTAs than anyone except Dame Judi Dench. But this is his first Lunchtime Achievement Award. We’ll be honouring Not The Nine O’Clock News, Blackadder, Spitting Image, and QI, all of which John originated.  On radio, he founded The News Huddlines, Quote…Unquote, and The News Quiz (now in its 49th year), and produced the pilot of To The Manor Born, which went on to huge tv success; and, for seventeen series, he hosted Radio 4’s The Museum of Curiosity, which won the 2016 International Rose D’Or for the world’s best radio talk show. John directed many TV ads – notably the Barclaycard series that become the Johnny English movie franchise – and wrote and directed Genesis: Land of Confusion, which won a Grammy and was No 1 in MTV’s Greatest Music Videos of All Time. John’s writing credits include the first series of The Hitchhiker’s Guide to the Galaxy with Douglas Adams, and over 30 books (including The Meaning of Liff with Douglas, still in print after 42 years). His one-man shows, Liff of QI (2013) and Emperor of the Prawns (2015), sold out at the Edinburgh Fringe.

JON CANTER

Comedy writer, playwright and novelist Jon Canter co-founded the Aldeburgh Comedy Weekend, and has lived in the town for over thirty years. His Radio 4 comedy Believe It, starring Richard Wilson, won Best Scripted Comedy at the BBC Audio Awards.  Last year’s Weekend featured the stage premiere of his tragi-comic play A Short Gentleman, based on his novel, and starring Michael Fenton Stevens. With John Lloyd, his contemporary in the Cambridge Footlights, he co-wrote Afterliff – The New Dictionary Of Things There Should Be Words For.

Aldeburgh Cinema: Desert Island Comedy Flicks with Dame Joanna Lumley 

Hosted by Clive Tulloh

Sunday 18th October, 5.30pm

Actress, activist, presenter and author, Joanna Lumley’s come a long way since her early film role in On Her Majesty’s Secret Service, in which she was credited simply as ‘The English Girl’. Made a Dame for her services to drama, entertainment and charity, she’s now an English Woman admired everywhere for her eloquence, wit and poise. Post Bond-Girl, she went on to appear in Curse Of The Pink Panther, Shirley Valentine, James And The Giant Peach, and The Wolf Of Wall Street. On tv she went from Purdey in The New Avengers, and Sapphire in Sapphire And Steel, to the immortal Patsy in Absolutely Fabulous, for which she was awarded two BAFTAS. She re-united with Jennifer Saunders for Jam & Jerusalem and then with Saunders and Lucy Punch for the current big hit, Amandaland. (She and Punch first met when they played stepmother and daughter in the Cinderella satire, Ella Enchanted.) At the Aldeburgh Cinema she’ll be choosing clips from her favourite comedy films in Desert Island Comedy Flicks, a variant on the long-running Desert Island Flicks strand, which has featured Lenny Henry, Richard Curtis & Emma Freud, and Michael Palin.

CLIVE TULLOH

TV producer Clive Tulloh and Joanna Lumley are no strangers to Desert Islands. Award winning Box Tale Soup present a stunning new adaptation of Orwell’s dystopian classic. Known for their ingenious sets and slick storytelling, Box Tale Soup’s unique puppetry becomes a striking metaphor for Orwell’s chilling vision of power and control.

 

The year is 1984, perhaps – it is impossible to tell for certain. We are being watched, Big Brother is everywhere, truth changes daily, and everyone conforms. About the Production

Box Tale Soup’s version of Lewis Carroll’s Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland has been created and adapted entirely by the company. It features a cast of just two human performers, a dozen colourful handmade puppets and a beautiful set that unfolds from a vintage trunk, alongside a magical soundtrack of original music composed especially for the show. From the surprising appearance of the Cheshire Cat to the madness of the Tea Party, this is a constantly inventive and refreshing take on Carroll’s classic work.

Alice’s Adventures in Wonderland follows Alice on her remarkable journey, from her fleeting encounters with the White Rabbit to her eventual showdown with the crazed Queen of Hearts. A confrontational Caterpillar, Ukelele twanging Tweedle twins and an array of others pop up along the way, before Alice comes face to face with her own imagination and perhaps the author of her dreams.

About the Hall

Nestling in the heart of the seaside town of Aldeburgh in Suffolk sits the historic and much-loved Jubilee Hall. Built in 1887 by local businessman Newson Garrett to celebrate Queen Victoria’s Golden Jubilee, the hall was intended for “concerts and dramatic entertainments provided by well-known artistes, dances for the little people on wet afternoons and for ‘the grown-ups’ in the evenings”. Ever since then the hall has offered an array of musical and dramatic performances, both amateur and professional and has also served the community as a film-theatre, dance-hall, badminton court and roller-skating rink. The Jubilee Hall is nothing if not adaptable.

At Jubilee Hall we are ambitious for our building and what goes on within it, ensuring we not only survive, but thrive, for decades to come. We work in partnership in Aldeburgh, Suffolk and beyond, from schools to businesses, from community groups to performing artists, providing excellence balanced by diversity in both programming and activities.

A Brief History

The Jubilee Hall gained unexpected fame in 1948 when local residents Benjamin Britten and Peter Pears chose it as a venue for their ‘Festival of Music and the Arts’. In 1960 the first ever performance of Britten’s A Midsummer Night’s Dream was given in the hall, and since then many notable opera premières have taken place there, such as Britten’s The Little Sweep, William Walton’s The Bear and Harrison Birtwistle’s Punch and Judy. Although the main venue of the Aldeburgh Festival has moved to Snape Maltings, many of the Festival events continue to take place in the Jubilee Hall.
